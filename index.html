3. Famous Performances & Tours

Super Bowl XXVII (1993) – Rose Bowl, Pasadena

Michael Jackson was 34 years old when he performed at Super Bowl XXVII on January 31, 1993, at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, California.

This performance changed entertainment history forever because it was the first halftime show to significantly increase viewership during the Super Bowl itself.

Setlist

  • Jam (intro from Why You Wanna Trip on Me)
  • Billie Jean (moonwalk performance)
  • Black or White
  • We Are the World (choir)
  • Heal the World

He stood still for nearly 90 seconds at the start, letting anticipation build — a moment that became iconic.

This performance transformed the Super Bowl halftime show into a global entertainment event, leading the NFL to begin hiring major superstar performers every year afterward.

Motown 25 – The Moonwalk (1983)

At age 25, Michael performed Billie Jean on Motown 25: Yesterday, Today, Forever. This is where he famously introduced the moonwalk to the world.

While he did not invent the move, his performance made it globally iconic and defined his legacy as a performer.

Major World Tours

Bad World Tour (1987–1989)

His first solo world tour, performing in front of over 4.4 million people. This tour established him as the biggest global solo entertainer in history at the time.

Dangerous World Tour (1992–1993)

Known for advanced stage effects and massive stadium crowds. This tour pushed his physical limits due to constant global travel and intense choreography.

HIStory World Tour (1996–1997)

His largest tour ever, performing 82 concerts in front of over 4.5 million fans worldwide. This tour was extremely demanding physically and emotionally due to nonstop pressure and media attention.

Impact of Touring on Michael

These tours made Michael Jackson the most successful live performer of his era, but they also caused exhaustion, stress, and perfectionism that affected his later career.

Despite the pressure, he continued to push creative boundaries in performance, staging, and storytelling, setting standards still used in modern concerts today.
